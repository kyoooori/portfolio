Overview

OpenGov’s Permitting & Licensing product (PLC) helps local governments run permits, licenses and code enforcement. Search is how staff get to nearly everything in it. In a single 30-day window in 2023, 8,070 users across 445 accounts clicked into search 870,000 times. It was also the feature people complained about most.

I led the redesign as the only designer, working with one PM and two engineers. We shipped in three months. The new search lives in the global navigation, and I designed it as a pattern that other OpenGov products can reuse.

Voice of a user

Problem space

The problem was pretty clear.

Very slow

The search function is described as slow or sluggish, which becomes particularly frustrating when users are on a call with a customer or need to quickly access information. High server usage times, such as around 3:00 PM, seem to make this worse.

“So sometimes I’ll be on the phone, I’m like, hang on. My system is a little slow and then I’m waiting for it to load and then I can go in and access what it is that they need, primarily in the inspection step.”Customer interview

Low relevance of results

It often yields too many results, making it difficult to find the most recent or relevant application or record. Users have to comb through multiple results, sometimes running into outdated information.

“Too many results at a time, and properties are at the bottom, which is often what we’re looking for.”Customer interview

Search isn’t searching

Users report that the search sometimes does not return the items they are looking for, even when the items exist in the system.

Inconsistent results

The search experience is inconsistent. For example, typing the exact record number does not always lead to the record showing up at the top of the results.

The hunt and peck

More often than not, our users are looking for a record. However, they think and organize information mentally in the form of addresses or locations.

Inflexible and rigid input

Users wish for more refined search capabilities, including the ability to filter by specific criteria like record number, address or applicant.

There are difficulties when searching for specific details like project names or addresses, with the system sometimes not recognizing them.

Additionally, our search is not able to identify intent if inputs are misspelled, which is an industry standard.

Inaccessible

The tabbing pattern in the current search doesn’t let you tab into the results once searched.

Additionally, the icons indicating the object type for a search result fail to meet WCAG color contrast standards.

Find faster

A Microsoft study revealed that we now have an attention span of eight seconds, shorter than that of a goldfish (nine seconds).

Visibility: metadata

Users want to see more results, and as much metadata as possible in search results. DBA or business name is a recurring ask from Licensing users. How do we design results that provide more metadata and still feel lightweight?

Flexibility: filters

Users want the ability to filter down their results and tell us more about what they’re looking for. However, the design must provide good quality results even when no filters are applied.
