Overview

In OpenGov’s Permitting & Licensing product, everything is a record: a permit, a license, a code complaint. But the records did not talk to each other. A building permit had no idea that an electrical permit was waiting on it, or that a Certificate of Occupancy could not be issued until both were done.

Customers had asked for this through every channel we had: Productboard, our ideas portal, sales calls and professional services meetings. Competitors already offered it. It had sat on the roadmap for a while and kept slipping, but as OpenGov moved up-market, connected records became table stakes. I was the designer on it from discovery through launch.

We identified our use cases

Special events permits

Handling complex processes involving multiple permits and licenses for special events, such as music festivals, to streamline the process and improve user experience.

Construction projects

The primary building permit acts as the parent, and other permits like electrical, plumbing and mechanical are child permits.

Retail and business

Permits at different levels (mall level, building level, unit level) need to be tracked and related.

Integration with existing systems

Setting up inspections and permits as tasks in an Enterprise Asset Management (EAM) system to trigger actions like snow removal.

Fee management and payment

Combining fees across multiple permits to allow a single payment process, enhancing the user experience.

And user goals

  • Record requirement
  • Permit hierarchy
  • Bulk permit and license tracking
  • Task dependencies

Components and measuring success

Everything is a component

KPIs: the honest answer

This project is greenfield. In conversations with the PM, we also found that a lot of the improvements we are building are qualitative. This is the list we came up with, but it is still a work in progress.

What we expect

1. Creation of ad-hoc related records
2. Creation of more relationship templates in larger communities
3. Reduction in use of combo-permits
4. Fewer questions about requirements via phone calls or emails
5. Faster processing of permits and licenses

What we could measure

1. Number of workflow steps in records (reduction is ideal)
2. Number of change requests (reduction is ideal)
3. Time to complete a workflow step (reduction is ideal, but we do not measure this today)
