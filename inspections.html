Setting up inspectors

Admins define who inspects what, and when. Each inspector has the inspection types they’re qualified for, the days they work, and their hours per day and week. That becomes the capacity the rest of the system schedules against.

Booking an inspection

Applicants book inspections themselves in a step-by-step flow: organize inspections, pick a date and time, add a contact and notes, then review. They choose a day, then a morning or afternoon slot, and the calendar shows which slots are open and which will need staff confirmation. Requests outside regular hours capture a label, a desired time and a reason, so staff can approve them.

Handling requests

Staff see new requests in one queue, with the record, when the request came in, and the applicant’s preferred time. Off-hour requests are flagged, and staff can see who else is viewing a request so two people don’t book the same one. Inspections booked automatically into a full day are called out, so a supervisor can step in.

Planning capacity

Supervisors plan by district. The week view shows morning and afternoon slots for every zone, flags the days that need more slots, and counts the floaters available to help. Capacity Planning goes a level deeper: which districts are full, which inspectors are booked, and which floaters can be added to a district with one click.

Scheduling

Booked inspections land on a shared calendar that can be filtered by inspector or group. Appointments that were booked automatically are marked, and the calendar syncs with Outlook, so inspectors see inspections and meetings in one place.

Doing the inspection

Inspections are steps in the record’s workflow, with a summary of how many have passed, partially passed, failed or are locked, plus completed and scheduled visits. In the field, the inspector sees which visit this is, gets context written by OG Assist, and records an overall result of pass, fail or partial pass, with comments, photos and checklist items. They can also cancel upcoming visits or ask for more.
